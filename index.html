🔍 What vault-mirror Does

If you keep notes in Obsidian, you already have a vault: a folder full of Markdown files that holds everything you have written down — meeting notes, research, snippets, journals, project plans. That folder is yours, and it lives on your own computer.

vault-mirror adds a second, invisible copy of your knowledge: a search index. This index is built with a tool called ruvector and it understands not just the exact words you typed, but what your notes are about. That is what people call semantic search — you can search for "how do we handle refunds" and find a note titled "Customer credit policy" even though the words do not match.

Every time you add, rename, or edit a note, vault-mirror notices and updates the index so the two stay in step. Your AI assistant then asks the index first, gets a short, relevant set of notes back, and answers using your own knowledge instead of guesswork. Nothing is uploaded. Nothing is sent to a server. Your notes stay on your machine, and vault-mirror is strictly read-only when it comes to your vault.

⚙️ How It Works

There is no complicated setup and no cloud account. In plain terms, this is the whole cycle:

  1. You point vault-mirror at your vault. You choose the folder where your Obsidian notes live. That is a one-time choice.
  2. It reads your notes. It opens each Markdown file, understands the content, and builds a local search index next to it.
  3. It keeps watching. When you save a new note or edit an old one, the index quietly catches up in the background.
  4. Your AI searches first. When you ask a question, the assistant looks in the index, finds the handful of notes that matter, and answers from them.
  5. You keep writing. Your vault remains a normal folder of normal text files. If you ever remove vault-mirror, your notes are exactly as you left them.

🖥️ Your First Run

Here is what a healthy first run looks like from your side, without any technical detail:

  • You pick the folder. vault-mirror confirms which vault it will watch.
  • It counts your notes. You see a number that roughly matches how many notes you think you have. A mismatch usually means you picked a folder one level too high or too low.
  • It finishes. A message tells you the index is ready and how many notes it read.
  • It stays quiet. From then on it sits in the background and keeps the index matching your vault. You do not need to babysit it.

If you write a note while it is indexing, that is fine. The note is picked up on the next pass, and nothing is lost.

🧠 Getting the Best Results

vault-mirror is only as useful as the notes it can see, so a few simple habits make a big difference.

  • Give notes real titles. A file called "policy" is harder to route to than "Refund policy for subscription customers".
  • Write in full sentences. Meaning-based search works from the content of your writing. A note that says only "TODO: check" gives it almost nothing to work with.
  • Keep one idea per note. Small, focused notes are easier to surface than one enormous page covering twenty topics.
  • Don't worry about folders. The index does not care where a file lives. It cares what the file says.
  • Let it catch up. If you paste in a hundred new notes at once, give it a moment before expecting them in results.

❓ Common Questions

Does vault-mirror change my notes?

No. It only reads. Your Markdown files stay exactly as they are, and deleting vault-mirror would leave your vault untouched.

Does anything leave my computer?

No. The vault and the index both live locally. There is no cloud account and no upload step.

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You choose a folder, let it index, and then use your AI assistant as usual. The release page includes the one configuration line needed to connect a command-line assistant, and it is a copy-and-paste job.

What if I have more than one vault?

Run vault-mirror once per vault. Each vault gets its own index, and they stay independent of each other.

What happens when I rename a note?

The index notices the old note is gone and the new one exists, so results stay accurate. Your files are never moved by vault-mirror itself.

Can I use it with tools other than Obsidian?

Yes, as long as the notes are Markdown files in a folder. Obsidian is simply the most common case.
